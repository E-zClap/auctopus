# Code of conduct

Participation in Auctopus should allow people to ask questions, contribute work and challenge technical claims without harassment. This policy sets expectations for that participation and explains how to raise a concern. Repository maintainers handle reports.

## Scope

This policy applies to users, contributors, reviewers and maintainers in repository issues, pull requests, code reviews and communications about project contributions. It also applies when someone represents Auctopus in another space.

The same expectations apply to public exchanges and private communications arranged to address a project concern. Experience, institutional affiliation and responsibility for the repository do not exempt anyone from these expectations.

## Expected behaviour

- Discuss ideas, evidence and proposed changes with respect. Explain a technical objection and give the other person room to respond.
- Ask for clarification when meaning or intent is uncertain. Allow for differences in experience, language and communication style.
- Make requests specific and proportionate to the task. Give contributors time to consider feedback and respect a decision to step away.
- Credit other people's work and describe your own contribution accurately. Distinguish observed results from assumptions when discussing research or software behaviour.
- Respect consent and privacy. Ask before publicly sharing someone else's correspondence, personal information or unpublished research material. Necessary evidence for a private conduct report is handled as described below.
- Accept corrections, acknowledge the effect of your actions and adjust your behaviour when a concern is explained.

Technical disagreement can be direct. Keep comments relevant to the work, explain the evidence behind your position and avoid assumptions about another person's motives or competence.

## Unacceptable behaviour

- Harassment, threats, intimidation or encouragement of these actions.
- Discriminatory remarks or exclusion based on personal characteristics, including race, nationality, disability, age, religion, gender or sexual orientation.
- Personal insults, sexualised comments, unwelcome sexual attention or repeated contact after someone has asked it to stop.
- Publishing another person's private information or correspondence without permission, or threatening to do so.
- Coercion or repeated pressure to obtain private prompts, instrument descriptions, credentials or unpublished research, or unauthorised disclosure of that material outside the private reporting process below.
- Repeated disruption of project discussions, including continuing personal attacks after a request to stop.
- Retaliation against someone for raising a concern, supplying evidence or participating in its review.

Good-faith questions about reproducibility and requests for public examples are welcome. Respect a refusal to share private material.

## Report a concern

Open a public blank issue in the [Auctopus repository](https://github.com/E-zClap/auctopus) asking maintainers to arrange a private contact. Include only that request. Do not describe the incident, identify anyone involved, or attach correspondence, screenshots or other evidence.

If the concern involves a maintainer, ask for a different maintainer in the contact request. Do not name anyone or include incident details. Wait until a private channel with an uninvolved maintainer has been arranged before sharing details.

For the private report, describe what happened, where it happened and any continuing concern. Include relevant links or short excerpts where available. Necessary evidence, including relevant correspondence, may be shared in good faith through that channel without the reported person's consent. Remove credentials and omit unrelated personal information or private research material.

## Handling reports

Repository maintainers assess reports and decide what action is appropriate within the project. They may ask for clarification or relevant context. A maintainer involved in a report must not decide the outcome of their own case.

Responses may include a request to change behaviour, removal of offending content, or a restriction on participation. Maintainers should consider the conduct, its effect and whether it has continued after an earlier intervention.

Report details should be shared only as needed to assess the concern and take action. Participants should avoid public speculation about reports and respect requests to stop behaviour while a concern is reviewed.
